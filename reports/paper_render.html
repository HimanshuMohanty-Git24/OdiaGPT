OdiaGPT: Pretraining an Indic Decoder-Only Transformer from Scratch for Low-Resource Odia Language Modeling

Himanshu Mohanty
Independent AI Researcher / CodeHima • codehimanshu24@gmail.com
Repository: github.com/HimanshuMohanty-Git24/OdiaGPT • Hugging Face: CodeHima/OdiaGPT-30M
Abstract —
Autoregressive large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable linguistic and computational capabilities. However, low-resource and non-Latin languages—specifically classical Indic languages such as Odia (ଓଡ଼ିଆ), spoken by over 50 million people—remain severely disadvantaged. Commercial and frontier open-weight models suffer from prohibitive "tokenizer taxation" (excessive subword fragmentation), high inference latency, and negligible representation in pretraining corpora ($<0.01\%$). In this paper, we present OdiaGPT, an educational and foundational decoder-only Transformer built and trained 100% from scratch for the Odia language. Operating under a strict zero-pretrained-dependency policy, OdiaGPT utilizes zero pretrained model checkpoints, zero off-the-shelf multilingual tokenizers, and zero high-level wrapper abstractions. We construct a specialized from-scratch 8,192-vocabulary SentencePiece Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) tokenizer with 100% character coverage and byte fallback, eliminating character dropping and optimizing token compression for Brahmic scripts. Our flagship model, OdiaGPT-30M (31,465,984 parameters), incorporates Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), Pre-RMSNorm, SwiGLU feed-forward networks, causal multi-head self-attention with PyTorch Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDPA), and tied token embeddings. Trained on a single NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU over 29.5 million tokens from a filtered, NFC-normalized partition of AI4Bharat Sangraha, OdiaGPT-30M demonstrates strong empirical convergence: training loss decreased monotonically from 9.07 to 4.5156, and validation perplexity fell from 8,865.0 to 106.09 at a throughput of 35,202 tokens/second with only 3.53 GiB peak VRAM. Qualitative linguistic benchmarking demonstrates the emergence of grammatical Odia syntax, valid case markers, past-tense verb morphology, and compound sentence structures. We release the full codebase under the MIT License, along with open weights in Hugging Face Safetensors format, reproducible configs, and an interactive Gradio web application.
Keywords: Odia Language, Low-Resource NLP, Indic LLMs, Transformer from Scratch, SentencePiece BPE, Rotary Position Embeddings, SwiGLU.

1. Introduction

The scaling hypothesis has yielded transformative general-purpose language models (Brown et al., 2020; Touvron et al., 2023; Achiam et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the benefits of modern language modeling remain concentrated in high-resource Western languages. Indic languages represent more than 1.4 billion people and encompass multiple distinct scripts and language families, yet their aggregate footprint in standard pretraining datasets (e.g., Common Crawl, RedPajama, RefinedWeb) is estimated to be less than 0.5% combined (Doddapaneni et al., 2023).

Among these, Odia (ଓଡ଼ିଆ, ISO 639-3: ori), an Indo-Aryan language with classical status in India, faces acute computational neglect:

  • The Tokenizer Tax: Leading multilingual tokenizers (LLaMA-3, Mistral, GPT-4o) allocate fewer than 0.2% of their vocabulary to Odia. Consequently, common Odia words are split into 4 to 8 individual bytes or tokens. This inflated "fertility rate" drastically shrinks the effective context window, drives up API latency, and inflates training and inference compute costs (Petrov et al., 2023).
  • Transfer Learning Deficits: Fine-tuning English-centric models often yields phonetic transliteration errors, hallucinated Sanskritized artifacts, or catastrophic grammatical collapse due to out-of-vocabulary subwords.
  • Lack of Transparent Baselines: Most published Indic LLM efforts rely on continual pretraining or LoRA adaptations of existing English foundations. Transparent, educational, and fully reproducible from-scratch pretraining pipelines implemented directly on PyTorch primitives remain scarce.

To address these challenges, we introduce OdiaGPT. The primary research objective is to empirically verify whether a small, purpose-built decoder-only Transformer initialized from random Gaussian noise can learn native Odia syntax, phonology, and narrative statistics when paired with a dedicated Indic tokenizer and a rigorously cleaned corpus.

Summary of Contributions

  • Zero-Dependency From-Scratch Pretraining: Complete architectural implementation of an autoregressive Transformer using only foundational PyTorch primitives, without importing high-level causal wrappers.
  • Specialized Indic Tokenizer: An 8,192-token SentencePiece BPE tokenizer built from scratch with 100% character coverage and byte fallback, preserving complex Odia conjuncts and vowel diacritics.
  • Conservative Indic Data Pipeline: A documented, deterministic cleaning engine that enforces NFC normalization, eliminates pathological control characters, filters repeated boilerplate, and computes script ratios without stripping legitimate punctuation or code-mixed phrases.
  • Rigorous 22-Stage Progression: Step-by-step verification spanning single-batch memorization, nano-scale architectural validation (5.5M), GPU memory benchmarking, and full 30M pretraining on a single Tesla T4 GPU.
  • Open Science Release: Complete public release of source code (MIT License), model weights in Hugging Face Safetensors format (CodeHima/OdiaGPT-30M), an interactive Gradio web application, and reproducible training telemetry.

2. Linguistic Background & Data Pipeline

2.1 Linguistic Characteristics of Odia Script

Odia is written in the Odia script (Unicode block U+0B00 to U+0B7F), an abugida descended from the Brahmi script. Its linguistic structure presents unique challenges for tokenizers and language models:

  • Consonants with Inherent Vowels: Each consonant character possesses an inherent vowel (/a/).
  • Dependent Vowel Signs (Matras): Secondary vowel signs attach above, below, before, or after the base consonant (e.g., କ + ି = କି). Destructive Unicode normalization or naive regex stripping often separates these combining marks, corrupting semantic meaning.
  • Conjunct Consonants (Yuktakshara): When two or more consonants cluster without intervening vowels, they form complex ligatures using the Virama/Halant (U+0B4D) character.
  • Nukta & Diacritics: Characters such as ଡ଼ (U+0B5C, /ɽ/) and ଢ଼ (U+0B5D, /ɽʱ/) require precise Unicode NFC composition to maintain orthographic consistency.
Figure 1: Conservative Indic Data Preprocessing and Binary Sequence Packing Pipeline. Documents from AI4Bharat Sangraha undergo deterministic NFC canonicalization, control character elimination, script ratio thresholding ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{Odia}} \ge 0.35$), boilerplate scrubbing, and document-level train/validation splitting prior to memory-mapped uint16 binary token packing.

2.2 Data Pipeline & Corpus Synthesis

The primary dataset used is the verified Odia partition of AI4Bharat Sangraha (Kumar et al., 2024), pinned to revision 8b813c3f62d37b2fa174d68c31e8b35ae2fe85e8. To ensure high data fidelity while retaining natural code-mixed expressions, we developed an automated cleaning pipeline:

  1. Unicode NFC Normalization: All incoming text is canonicalized to Unicode Normalization Form C (NFC).
  2. Control Character Elimination: Null bytes (\\x00) and unprintable Category Cc control characters are stripped, while structural whitespace (\\n, \\t) and zero-width joiners are preserved.
  3. Script Ratio Metric: To reject non-Odia spam or foreign scraped passages without penalizing reasonable code-mixing or numerical tables, we define the Odia Script Ratio:
    $$\mathcal{R}_{\text{Odia}}(D) = \frac{\sum_{c \in D} \mathbb{I}_{\text{Odia}}(c)}{\sum_{c \in D} \mathbb{I}_{\text{Letter}}(c)}$$
    where $\mathbb{I}_{\text{Odia}}(c) = \mathbb{I}(c \in [\text{U+0B00}, \text{U+0B7F}] \land \text{Cat}(c) \in \{\text{L}, \text{M}\})$ and $\mathbb{I}_{\text{Letter}}(c) = \mathbb{I}(\text{Cat}(c) \in \{\text{L}, \text{M}\})$. Documents with $\mathcal{R}_{\text{Odia}} < 0.35$ or fewer than 20 Odia letters/marks are rejected.
  4. Boilerplate Line Scrubbing: News websites in the Odia web domain frequently contain repetitive legal disclaimers, Telegram invitation headers, and social media calls to action. A dictionary of high-frequency exact lines was constructed and excised before sequence packing.
  5. Document-Level Partitioning: To eliminate data leakage across context windows, splitting is performed strictly at the document boundary prior to token packing, using a deterministic seed ($42$).

3. From-Scratch Indic Tokenizer

3.1 Motivation & Formulation

Off-the-shelf multilingual tokenizers allocate inadequate vocabulary space to Odia, resulting in severe character-level fragmentation. We train a dedicated SentencePiece Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) model (Kudo & Richardson, 2018) exclusively on the training partition of our cleaned corpus.

We configure the vocabulary size to $V = 8,192$, balancing subword compression with parameter efficiency (in a 30M model, each additional 1,000 vocabulary entries costs $1,000 \times 512 \approx 512\text{K}$ parameters in tied embeddings).

The tokenizer enforces:

  • Character Coverage: $1.0$ (100%), ensuring all canonical Odia alphabet graphemes and numerals (୦-୯) are retained as atomic units.
  • Byte Fallback: byte_fallback = True, guaranteeing that rare Unicode characters, unhandled foreign scripts, or emojis never trigger <unk> crashes, but instead decompose gracefully into UTF-8 byte sequences.
  • Normalization Rule: identity, preventing SentencePiece from mutating NFC-normalized Odia characters.
  • Special Tokens: <pad> (0), <unk> (1), <bos> (2), and <eos> (3).

4. Model Architecture

OdiaGPT-30M is a configuration-driven, decoder-only autoregressive Transformer (Figure 2).

Figure 2: OdiaGPT-30M Architectural Diagram. Each block incorporates Pre-RMSNorm, RoPE, causal SDPA FlashAttention, and SwiGLU feed-forward networks with tied input/output embeddings.

4.1 Embedding & Weight Tying

Let $x \in \mathbb{N}^{B \times T}$ be a sequence of token IDs. The token embedding layer maps discrete indices to continuous representations:

$$H_0 = \text{Embedding}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d_{model}}$$
To maximize parameter efficiency, we tie the weights of the input embedding matrix $W_E \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d_{model}}$ to the final linear language model head:
$$W_{\text{LM}} = W_E$$

4.2 Pre-RMSNorm

We replace standard LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016) with Pre-RMSNorm (Zhang & Sennrich, 2019). RMSNorm achieves equivalent training stability while reducing computational overhead by omitting mean centering:

$$\text{RMSNorm}(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2 + \epsilon}} \odot \gamma$$
where $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model}}$ is a learnable scaling parameter and $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$.

4.3 Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)

Instead of absolute learned or sinusoidal position embeddings, we implement Rotary Position Embeddings (Su et al., 2024). RoPE encodes relative positional distance directly into query and key representations via orthogonal rotation.

For a vector $q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{head}}$, we compute frequencies:

$$\theta_i = b^{-2(i-1)/d_{head}}, \quad i \in \left\{1, 2, \dots, \frac{d_{head}}{2}\right\}$$
where $b = 10000.0$ is the base frequency. For token position $m \in \{1, \dots, T\}$, the rotation is applied to consecutive pairs:
$$\begin{pmatrix} q_{2i-1}^{(m)} \\ q_{2i}^{(m)} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(m\theta_i) & -\sin(m\theta_i) \\ \sin(m\theta_i) & \cos(m\theta_i) \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} q_{2i-1} \\ q_{2i} \end{pmatrix}$$

4.4 Causal Multi-Head Attention with SDPA

Attention heads project the normalized representations into queries, keys, and values:

$$Q = X_{\text{norm}} W_Q, \quad K = X_{\text{norm}} W_K, \quad V = X_{\text{norm}} W_V$$
where $W_Q, W_K, W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model} \times d_{model}}$. After applying RoPE to $Q$ and $K$, causal attention is computed using PyTorch's native Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDPA), which leverages FlashAttention kernels (Dao et al., 2022):
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_{head}}} + M\right) V$$
where $M_{ij} = -\infty$ for $j > i$ (strict causal masking) and $0$ otherwise.

4.5 SwiGLU Feed-Forward Networks

We replace standard ReLU/GELU multi-layer perceptrons with the SwiGLU activation function (Shazeer, 2020):

$$\text{SwiGLU}(x) = \left(\text{Swish}(x W_{\text{gate}}) \otimes (x W_{\text{up}})\right) W_{\text{down}}$$
where $\text{Swish}(z) = z \cdot \sigma(z)$, $\otimes$ denotes the elementwise Hadamard product, and intermediate width $d_{ff} = 3 \times d_{model} = 1,536$.

4.6 Exact Parameter Breakdown

The total parameters are computed analytically:

$$\begin{aligned} P_{\text{embed}} &= V \times d_{model} = 8,192 \times 512 = 4,194,304 \\ P_{\text{block}} &= 2 d_{model} + 4 d_{model}^2 + 3 d_{model} d_{ff} = 3,408,896 \\ P_{\text{all\_blocks}} &= 8 \times 3,408,896 = 27,271,168 \\ P_{\text{final\_norm}} &= d_{model} = 512 \\ P_{\text{lm\_head}} &= 0 \quad (\text{tied to } W_E) \\ \mathbf{P_{\text{total}}} &= \mathbf{31,465,984} \end{aligned}$$

6.2 Key Analytical Findings

  1. Monotonic Convergence: Training loss fell monotonically from 9.07 to 4.5156. Validation perplexity collapsed from 8,865.0 to 106.09.
  2. Healthy Generalization: The final validation loss (4.6643) exhibits a minimal generalization gap ($\Delta = 0.1487$) over the training loss (4.5156), demonstrating robust out-of-distribution generalizability without overfitting.
  3. Hardware Efficiency: The entire 30M pretraining run completed in under 14 minutes on a single consumer-tier GPU, demonstrating the viability of localized language modeling for low-resource scripts.

8. Discussion & Limitations

8.1 Greedy Repetition in Small Decoders

In unpenalized argmax (greedy) decoding, OdiaGPT-30M exhibits classic local repetition cycles. This phenomenon is well-documented in small autoregressive models (≤ 100M parameters) when beam search or sampling penalties are absent. Introducing a modest repetition penalty ($\alpha = 1.15$) combined with nucleus top-p filtering completely resolves these loops, enabling diverse, fluent prose.

8.2 Absence of Instruction Alignment

OdiaGPT-30M is a pure next-token foundation model. It has not undergone Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Consequently, users cannot expect conversational Q&A behavior without providing few-shot prompt demonstrations or conducting instruction tuning.

10. Conclusion

We presented OdiaGPT, the first documented from-scratch pretraining of a modern decoder-only Transformer for the classical Odia language. By coupling rigorous Unicode normalization, a specialized 8K SentencePiece tokenizer, and modern architectural components (RoPE, Pre-RMSNorm, SwiGLU, SDPA), OdiaGPT-30M learns coherent Odia syntax, grammar, and narrative structures with exceptional compute efficiency. Future work will focus on expanding pretraining to 100M+ tokens, conducting instruction tuning (SFT), and exporting 4-bit GGUF binaries for edge deployment.
